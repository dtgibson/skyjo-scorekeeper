Two pages, one spine.

Opened flat, iPhone Duo's inner display runs its fold straight through the middle of the 600pt column every screen uses today, so the pinned buttons sit on the crease. The proposal treats the open display as a book: the list keeps the left page, the heading and the primary action take the right page, and nothing tappable ever touches the spine. Standard iPhones and iPad have no fold region and stay exactly as they are.

1List left, action right

When the window reports a vertical fold, today's screen splits into its three bands: the heading goes top-right, the scrolling list takes the whole left page, the primary action pins bottom-right. Same type, same colours, same cards.

2The fold is the divider

Only if the system sheet straddles the crease: the score entry sheet's own column divider snaps to the fold, rows on one side, numpad, chips and Confirm on the other. Nothing is built if the system already keeps the sheet to one page.

3Lists may cross, controls may not

Tabletop keeps today's tall layout: the fold crosses the scrolling middle, the buttons already sit on the lower half. Closed, the outer display is a tall window above the 575pt threshold and changes nothing.
